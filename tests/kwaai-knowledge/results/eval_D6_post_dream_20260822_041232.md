The NEUM's 10 Point Programme included demands such as the full and unfettered franchise (the right to elect and be elected to all the councils of the state), free and equal education to the age of sixteen, and the right to elect and be elected to all the councils of the state [17].

### q34 — What was the Group Areas Act and how did it affect District Six?

According to excerpt [2], the Group Areas Act was a piece of legislation that played havoc with the lives of ordinary people in Cape Town, particularly in District Six. The Act declared Coloured Areas to be White, causing house prices to plummet as Coloured owners were forced to accept low offers from White purchasers. The Group Areas Board's valuation of properties was generally below market value, further exacerbating the problem.

Excerpt [3] states that the Non-White group most aggrieved by the Group Areas Act was the South African Indians. The Act effectively disenfranchised Coloured people by making them lose their municipal franchise when they moved to sub-economic houses in townships like Bonteheuwel, Hanover Park, and Mitchell's Plain (excerpt [14]).

Excerpt [5] mentions that the Group Areas Act was an attempt by the government to anticipate the wishes of the authorities, rather than genuinely addressing the needs of the community. The Act was part of a broader strategy of forced removals and social engineering, aimed at creating separate and segregated communities.

The Group Areas Act had a devastating impact on District Six, causing the haemorrhaging of the community as shops and residents left the area (excerpt [2]). The Act was a key factor in the eventual demolition of District Six, which was one of the most vibrant and diverse communities in Cape Town.

Overall, the Group Areas Act was a pivotal piece of legislation that had far-reaching consequences for District Six and its residents, contributing to the forced removals, displacement, and marginalization of the community.

### q40 — What was the Unity Movement's boycott policy?

The Unity Movement's boycott policy was to refuse to participate in any apartheid government institutions, including the Coloured Advisory Council (CAC).
