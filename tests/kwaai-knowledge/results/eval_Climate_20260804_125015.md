### q01 — According to Armstrong McKay et al., how many degrees of global warming could trigger multiple climate tipping points, and what does "tipping point" mean in this context?

According to the sources [2], [6], [13], [16], and [18], Armstrong McKay et al. state that:

* Multiple climate tipping points could be triggered at 1.5°C global warming or above.
* Tipping points are defined as "a critical threshold at which a tiny perturbation can qualitatively alter the state or development of a system" ([1] in source [16]).

This definition is further explained in source [18], where it is stated that a tipping point is a threshold in a forcing control parameter at which a small additional perturbation (within natural variability of ~0.2°C) causes a qualitative change significantly larger than the standard deviation of natural variability ([1] and SM for the full definition).

### q02 — What is CMIP6, according to Eyring et al., and what is its stated purpose in climate science?

According to [2], CMIP (Coupled Model Intercomparison Project) has evolved from a centralized activity to a federated one, with CMIP6 being the sixth phase. CMIP6 comprises 21 individual CMIP6-Endorsed MIPs and the DECK and CMIP6 historical simulations.

The stated purpose of CMIP6 is not explicitly mentioned in [2]. However, [3] states that CMIP6 will address three broad questions:

1. How does the Earth system respond to forcing?
2. What are the origins and consequences of systematic model biases?
3. How can we assess future climate changes given internal climate variability, predictability, and uncertainties in scenarios?

[5] adds that CMIP has adopted a new, more federated organizational structure to maximize the relevance and impact of CMIP6, which will address three broad questions:

1. How does the Earth system respond to forcing?
2. What are the origins and consequences of systematic model biases?
3. How can we assess future climate changes given internal climate variability, predictability, and uncertainties in scenarios?

The stated purpose of CMIP6 is to advance basic research in climate science, address a large range of specific questions, and fill the scientific gaps of previous CMIP phases (as mentioned in [15] and [17]).

### q05 — What is GISTEMP, per Lenssen et al., and what does the paper's "observational uncertainty ensemble" attempt to quantify?

According to the provided sources:

GISTEMP is a product that spans the possible regional and monthly uncertainty while properly accounting for the underlying spatiotemporal correlation structure [11].
It is also mentioned in source [6] as "global land-ocean analysis" which is formed by merging LSAT (Land Surface Air Temperature) with Sea Surface Temperature data from NOAA's Extended Reconstructed Sea Surface Temperature version 5 (ERSSTv5).

The paper's "observational uncertainty ensemble" attempts to quantify the observational uncertainty in studies that rely on historical temperature data [3, 10]. Specifically, it aims to account for possible errors arising from the adjustment of single station records to correct artificial break points due to changes in observing practices [4].
