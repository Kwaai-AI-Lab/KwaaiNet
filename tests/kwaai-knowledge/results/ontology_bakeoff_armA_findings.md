# Ontology bakeoff — arm A findings (2026-08-25)

Test data for [`PerKBOntology-plan.md`](../../../projects/kwaai-knowledge/plans/PerKBOntology-plan.md) §5.
Generated by `ontology_baseline.py` reading all 16 built graphs directly. No eval
run, no rebuild, ~7 seconds.

Raw: `ontology_baseline_20260825_120455.{md,json}`

---

## 1. The plan's arm A is mis-specified — it has never been run

`PerKBOntology-plan.md` §5.2 defines arm A as "today's global `ENTITY_TYPES` +
`RELATION_TYPES`". **No KB was built that way.** `multi_corpus_graph_build.sh`
carries a hand-tuned per-KB entity-type restriction:

```bash
[Climate]="Organization,Legislation,Publication"
[Legal]="Person,Organization,Legislation"
[Poems]="Person,Place"
```

So the 2026-08-22 baseline is already a **degenerate arm B** — a per-KB declared
entity ontology, expressed in bash, unversioned, unpersisted, entity-types-only,
with no relation vocabulary and no axioms.

This is the plan's central thesis showing up as an accident of tooling: someone
already found per-KB entity typing necessary enough to hand-maintain it in a
shell script. The plan's contribution is less "introduce per-KB ontology" than
**"finish the one that already exists and move it somewhere durable."**

Consequence for the bakeoff: either re-run a true unrestricted control, or
redefine arm A as "the bash declaration as it stands". The latter is cheaper and
more honest — it is the real baseline everything else was measured against.

## 2. Entity types were tuned per KB; relation types never were

That asymmetry is the whole finding. Every KB got a curated entity vocabulary
and all of them got the same 35 relation predicates, 14 of which are kinship.

## 4. The dominant predicate is `located_in`, not `associated_with`

This corrects the parent framing doc, which states "every relation dream
extracts is `associated_with`".

| kb | escape-hatch (`associated_with`+`related_to`) |
|---|---|
| CountryHistory | 44.1% |
| Astrophysics | 34.8% |
| WarPeace | 32.4% |
| MobyDick | 31.6% |
| Manhattan | 18.8% |
| D6 | 8.5% |

`located_in` alone is 54% of WarPeace and 51% of MobyDick. So the graphs are
predominantly **spatial**, not associational. The conclusion the framing doc drew
still holds — these edges do not carry domain meaning — but the mechanism is
different, and "the graph is a gazetteer" is a more accurate description than
"the graph is a bag of `associated_with`".

## 5. Two deficits, not one — and the bakeoff must separate them

For Climate the declared types are Organization/Legislation/Publication. The
*applicable* predicates already present in `RELATION_TYPES` are `cites`,
`part_of`, `contains`, `described_in`, `defined_by`, `supports`, `contradicts`,
`implements`, `associated_with` — **nine usable predicates**. Climate produced
**four relations from 1979 entities**.

So the mismatch is not only that the vocabulary lacks the right predicates. Even
the applicable ones barely fire. RFCs is the cleanest case: `cites` is exactly
the right predicate for an RFC corpus, it is in the vocabulary, and it fired
8 times across 1928 entities.

**Two hypotheses, and they demand different fixes:**

- **H1 — vocabulary.** The ontology offers nothing assertable, so nothing is
  asserted. Fixed by arms B/C.
- **H2 — extraction.** Applicable predicates exist but the extractor does not
  find them. Not fixed by any ontology.

The bakeoff's real job is to separate these. **If arm B or C gives Climate a
domain ontology and relation density stays near zero, the problem is H2 and the
ontology work will not fix it.** That is the falsifiable prediction worth
recording before running the arms.

Poems is the existing evidence for H2: same declared types as no-relation KBs
would predict a floor, but Poems has Person+Place — the same pair that gives
WarPeace 0.205 — and returns 0.0006. Declared types set a *ceiling* on which
predicates are applicable; the corpus and the extractor decide whether they fire.

## 6. Ten of the 17 global entity types are never used

Across 58,000 entities in 16 graphs, only six types ever appear: Person,
Organization, Place, Publication, Legislation. Never produced: `Event`,
`Concept`, `Method`, `Claim`, `Quantity`, `Date`, `Document`, `Technology`,
`Role`, `Topic`, `Unknown`.

Caveat: this is largely downstream of the bash restriction, so it is **not**
evidence the extractor cannot produce them. What it does show is that whoever
tuned the script never reached for them — including for corpora
(Climate, NIST, PythonDocs) where `Quantity`, `Method` and `Claim` are the
obvious types. Worth understanding before assuming a richer vocabulary helps.

## 7. Method notes

- KBs must be resolved through `~/.kwaainet/config.yaml`'s `rag_kbs` map, not by
  globbing `graph-*.db`. D6's directory holds two graph databases and the stale
  one is empty; globbing silently reports D6 as having zero entities.
- `graph score` is excluded throughout — it is hardcoded to the memoir ontology
  (plan §1.5) and cannot compare arms.

## 8. Live confirmation of the reproducibility hazard

Climate was induced twice, minutes apart, with an identical prompt and an
identical fixed chunk sample (`seed=17`), at `temperature=0.2`:

| run | proposed ents | proposed rels | grounded rels | duplicate predicates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| first | 8 | 13 | 9 | 0 |
| second | 9 | 15 | 15 | 6 |

Same input, materially different schema. This is the hazard §8.7 of the plan
records from KONDA, observed directly rather than cited. It is the reason an
induced ontology must be a **draft that is reviewed and committed**, never a
step re-run at ingest time — two rebuilds would otherwise silently produce two
different graphs.
